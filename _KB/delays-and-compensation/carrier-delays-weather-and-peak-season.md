# Carrier Delays, Weather, and Peak Season

**Summary.** Weather, carrier backlogs, and peak-season volume can delay delivery after an order has
shipped. These are outside {{STORE_NAME}}'s control, but the customer still gets the **goodwill delay
credit** ([DELAY-001](delay-compensation-tiers.md)) if it's late, and the lost-package flow applies
if it stops moving.

## Rule details
- Acknowledge the cause honestly; don't over-promise a date the carrier controls.
- Goodwill delay tiers still apply by **days late** ([DELAY-002](how-lateness-is-calculated.md)),
  regardless of cause.
- IF tracking stalls with no movement past the expected window → move to lost-package handling
  ([SHIP-006](../shipping-and-delivery/lost-or-stolen-packages.md)).
- Expedited/express fee that badly missed its promise → consider refunding the **upgrade fee** (see
  [SHIP-001](../shipping-and-delivery/shipping-methods-and-costs.md)); escalate if beyond authority.

## Examples
1. Shipped, delayed 4 days by a snowstorm → **$5** goodwill credit + honest explanation.
2. Peak-season backlog, 7 days late → **$15** goodwill credit.
3. No scans for days past the window → lost-package flow (reship/refund).

## What the agent must not do
- Do not invent a delivery date the carrier hasn't given.
- Do not deny the goodwill credit just because the carrier, not the store, caused the delay.

## Escalation
Express-fee refunds beyond authority, stalled tracking at the lost threshold, or value over $100 →
escalate.

## Customer-facing wording
> "There's a weather delay in the carrier network affecting your area, so it's running a few days
> behind. I've added a $5 credit for the trouble, and I'm watching the tracking — if it stops
> moving, I'll reship or refund."
