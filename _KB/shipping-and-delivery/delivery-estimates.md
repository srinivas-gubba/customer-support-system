# Delivery Estimates

**Summary.** The delivery estimate shown **at checkout** is the **promised delivery date** used for
lateness and goodwill credits. Estimates are representations under FTC law, so the agent gives honest
ranges and never invents a date.

## Rule details
- Estimate = processing (1–2 business days) + transit (standard 3–7 / expedited 2–3 / overnight 1).
- The checkout estimate is the **promised delivery date** ([DELAY-002](../delays-and-compensation/how-lateness-is-calculated.md)).
- Shipping/delivery claims must have a **reasonable basis** (FTC, [LEGAL-001](../legal/compliance-notes.md)).

## Examples
1. Standard order placed Mon → estimate ≈ **next Tue–the following Mon** (processing + 3–7 transit).
2. "Will it arrive by Friday?" 3 business days away on standard → honestly say it's tight; suggest
   expedited.

## What the agent must not do
- Do not promise a date earlier than processing + transit allows.
- Do not state a delivery date a tool didn't provide — escalate if unknown.

## Escalation
System can't produce an estimate, or a customer needs a guaranteed date the store can't commit to →
escalate.

## Customer-facing wording
> "For order #{id}, the estimate at checkout was {date}. That's processing plus carrier transit. If
> you need it sooner next time, expedited is 2–3 business days."
